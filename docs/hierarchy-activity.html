Design · Hierarchy browser

Every scope's activity, exact, in two milliseconds.

The theme proposal gives every scope row a meter for the share of its signals that change in the visible time range. That needs a yes or no for every signal in the design, on every pan and zoom. A signal is quiet in a window only when the window fits inside one of its silences, and silences longer than the window are rare. Keeping only silences longer than 102.4 ns turns each signal's history into a short stretch list. On openC910 it answers every window at least that wide exactly, for all 67,144 signals in 1.5 ms, from 1.8 MiB: 0.73% of the trace.

The problem

The meter asks about every signal, on every frame.

The wave panel only loads the signals on its canvas. A scope meter covers every signal below the scope, so the top of the tree covers the whole design: on C910, 67,144 signals behind 204,905 variables in 6,958 scopes. The question is the same for each of them: does this signal change between t0 and t1? The answer is needed again whenever the window moves.

The VTR reader can answer it only by decoding. Measured on one thread, a 100 ns window takes 0.14 s, because every dirty column of the overlapping block must be decompressed. The whole run takes 6.9 s, or 0.99 s on 16 threads. The 21 s quoted in the theme proposal came from vtr dump, which also formats every value. The only summary in the file is the dirty-group index of each block. It is free, but its groups hold 256 signals and its blocks span 777–2,068 ns. For a 100 ns window it marks 91% of all signals as possibly active when 26% are.

So the question is whether some small structure can answer exactly, for all signals, in a few milliseconds, at any zoom. If it cannot, what is the smallest compromise?

The idea

A window is quiet only inside a silence.

  1. Quiet means contained.A signal has no change in [t0, t1] exactly when its last change before t0 and its first change after t1 bound a silence that contains the window. The silences of one signal are disjoint, so at most one of them can hold a given window. That makes the question a lookup of one silence per signal, not a scan of changes.
  2. Only long silences matter.A window w wide fits only in a silence at least w + 2 time units long. Keep every silence longer than Δ and drop the rest. For any window at least Δ − 1 wide, the kept set holds every silence that could contain it, so the answer is exact, not an estimate.
  3. What is left is a stretch list.Between the long silences a signal is busy. Each busy stretch keeps three numbers: its first change, its last change, and its largest inner gap. A clock is one stretch with gap 1. A configuration register written once is one stretch of length zero. The 24,859 C910 signals that never change after the first time step have no stretches at all.
  4. Four comparisons per signal.Binary search for the first stretch that ends at or after t0. If there is none, or it starts after t1, the window sits in a silence and the signal is quiet. If the window holds the stretch's first or last change, the signal is active. So it is if the window is wider than the stretch's largest inner gap, as every window of at least Δ is. Otherwise, and only in a window narrower than Δ, the signal is undecided.
  5. Undecided signals are read, locally.A window narrower than Δ overlaps at most two of C910's 36 blocks, so the reader only has to read the undecided signals' columns in those blocks: 6–16 ms on 16 threads. Decoding cost grows with window width, and the index is exact above Δ. Each method is cheap exactly where the other is expensive.
  6. Scopes count distinct signals.67% of C910's variables alias a signal recorded elsewhere, so a scope counts signals, not variables. From each changing signal, walk up from the scopes of its variables and stop at the first scope already stamped with that signal. That is 826,739 steps with every signal active, 0.4 ms for a 100 ns window. Hui's colour-set-size method (+1 per occurrence, −1 at the common ancestor of consecutive occurrences) does the same in linear time, if it is ever needed.

Proof of the floor

No exact index can store less.

Slide a window of width w along one signal and record where the answer turns from active to quiet and back. Those turning points are exactly the ends of its silences longer than w + 1. So any structure that answers exactly for every window at least w wide must be able to reproduce the endpoints of every such silence. It can encode them in fewer bits, as the compressed column below does, but it cannot keep fewer of them. The same holds for the per-scope counts, because the root's count changes at each of those endpoints. The stretch list stores those endpoints and nothing more, plus one gap per stretch.

The count of long silences is therefore the real price of exactness, and C910 publishes it. Silences longer than one time unit number 594 million, 99.6% of the changes, so an exact index for one-unit windows is the trace again. Past 102.4 ns only 896,325 silences are left, 0.15% of the changes. Over that range the count falls roughly as 1/Δ. So exact answers for the narrowest windows must come from the trace, and an index can only make wide windows cheap. That is why the design uses both. Narrow windows are the only ones in which reading the trace is cheap.

A query cannot do much better either. Every signal must be looked at unless a structure also stores per-scope answers, and those have the same endpoints multiplied by the depth of the tree. One binary search per signal is 1.5 ms on one thread. That is well inside a frame and leaves no reason to add structure.

102.4 ns keeps the index under 1% of the trace, and the shortest block spans 777 ns, so a window narrower than Δ touches at most two blocks. Only 1.3% of signals are undecided at 100 ns, and 22–24% at a nanosecond or less. At that zoom they are resolved in 6–16 ms on 16 threads, or 33–76 ms on one, through today's per-signal reads.

"Intervals kept" counts one leading and one trailing silence per signal, and "in memory" is the measurement layout of 16 bytes per interval. Stored as 32-bit start and end plus a 16-bit gap, with signals of identical history sharing one list, Δ = 102.4 ns needs about 6.4 MiB: C910 has 24,233 distinct lists for 67,144 signals. Built from decoded changes, the index takes 0.63 s on one thread. In the reader it is fused into one parallel pass over the blocks, which costs 0.99 s on 16 threads.

Architecture

Where each piece lives.

  1. The reader owns the query."Which signals change in this window" is trace data, so it belongs in vtr, beside changes and for_each_change. The reader builds a StretchIndex in one pass that scans each column's time indices without merging them. It keeps the index as an immutable, shared, lazily built cache, the same ownership as its time tables. The C API projects it one to one.
  2. Two calls, one answer.classify fills an active set and an undecided set from the index in about 1.5 ms. resolve reads only the undecided signals' columns in the one or two blocks the window touches, decompressing each run once. A caller that wants a single exact answer calls both. The hierarchy browser shows the first answer at once and the second when it lands.
  3. Δ follows the file.The build keeps a log-scale histogram of silence lengths. It picks the smallest power of two that keeps the index under 1% of the trace and under a quarter of the shortest block. For C910 that is 102.4 ns. A trace with sparser activity gets a smaller Δ for free.
  4. Built in the background.Volna asks for the index through a new LoadRequest::StretchIndex on the load worker, like the analog and group summaries. Until it arrives, rows show counts without meters. On C910 that is about one second after open.
  5. Local and remote alike.The index is raw trace data, not presentation. The remote transport sends its compressed bytes once (1.8 MiB for C910), so panning never waits on the network. Meters, counts and the signal-to-scope walk stay client-side in volna-core.
  6. One pass per frame.When the wave viewport changes, the hierarchy model classifies once per frame, coalesced, off the UI thread, and counts all scopes with the stamp walk (≈2 ms together). Only the visible rows are painted. The signal-to-scope table is built once with the hierarchy.
  7. Honest while pending.In a window narrower than Δ, the meter draws the decided share solid and the undecided share hatched, as in the demo. A quiet scope is drawn faint only when its upper bound is zero. When resolve returns, the meter snaps to the exact value. A result for a window that has since moved is dropped.
  8. Semantics.A change is a recorded value change after the trace's first time step. Counting the initial values would make all 67,144 signals active in any window that touches the first time step. A scope counts the distinct signals below it, including subscopes. Signals with no change at all are part of the total and never light the meter.
  9. Not undoable.Meters are derived view state. Following the undo rules, they stay out of the journal, and they add no setting.
  10. Tested headlessly.core/vtr/tests checks classify plus resolve against a brute-force scan over random windows, including windows that end exactly on changes and block boundaries. A volna-core test checks the scope counts and the pending range. This page's test checks the demo against exact counts computed from the trace.

Later, if a background pass becomes too slow: an optional section. The writer's block encoder already walks every change. Keeping silences longer than Δ adds one subtraction and one comparison per change, on the encoder threads rather than the simulator's. Written per block, as each block's stretches plus its first and last change per signal, it costs 5.87 MiB against 5.76 MiB for one list over the whole run at Δ = 25.6 ns, so streaming and recovery keep working for 2% more space. The reader would then load the index instead of building it. That is a format change under AGENTS.md: a SPEC code, a round-trip test, a RATIONALE entry and an A/B of write time. It pays for traces whose parallel pass takes several seconds, about ten times C910, or for servers that open many traces. Until one exists, the reader-built index gives the same answers with no format change.

Limits

What it costs, stated plainly.

Windows under Δ are not free. In a window narrower than 102.4 ns, up to a quarter of the signals need the trace, and the exact meter arrives 6–16 ms after the window stops. The floor above shows this cannot be removed: exactness at one time unit costs the trace itself. A run-level resolve that caches the decompressed runs of the current block would make later pans inside it cost only a column scan. This has not been measured yet.

Memory scales with long silences. About 6.4 MiB packed for C910. That grows with run length and signal count, not with how busy the busy signals are. A ten times longer run of the same design needs about ten times as much, and the 1% rule raises Δ before memory gets out of hand.

Open costs one pass. 0.99 s on 16 threads, 6.9 s on one, in the background. The optional section removes it at the price of a format change.

Yes or no, not how much. The meter shows how many signals change, not how often. Activity strength would need change counts per stretch, a different question with a different floor, and this design does not answer it.

Research

Nobody answers this exactly today.

None of the open viewers we read, and no commercial manual we could find, has a per-scope activity display or an exact query for which signals change in a range. The closest pieces of prior art are listed here.

FST stores one position per variable per value-change block, zero when the variable has no changes there. That is the per-block bit of the options table, at block resolution. PulseView keeps ten mipmap levels of ×16, each entry the OR of its children's change bits, and uses them to skip ahead while drawing. Perfetto summarises counters as min, max and last per bucket. Trace Compass's state history tree stores state intervals on disk for time-range queries, the storage closest to a list of silences. Surfer and wellen load only the signals on the canvas.

SAIF gives power tools per-net toggle counts and time at 0, 1 and X, per instance, but for one fixed window per file. Emulation power tools profile activity over billions of cycles to find the busy windows, then analyse only those. The range filters Memento, Grafite and Rosetta answer range emptiness with at least log2(R/ε) bits per key. That fits when keys are few and ranges short. Here the keys are 597 million changes. The stretch index turns the problem round: it keeps the complement, the few long silences, and its negatives are exact. Elias–Fano coding is the natural packing if the stretch lists ever need to shrink further. Distinct counting over the tree is Hui's colour set size problem (CPM 1992).

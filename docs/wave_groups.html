Signal groups in the waveform panel

Put rows under a named heading, nest headings, and fold them away. A folded group still shows when and where its signals change, so you can close it without losing sight of it.

Design, implemented in volna-core and the GPUI viewer; the plan records what was built and where it differs, and docs/RATIONALE.md (“Volna signal groups”) the measurements. The mock below is the design as proposed. Before groups, the Waves panel was a flat list of signal, lane and clock rows. A debug session on a real SoC collects dozens of them, and they could only be organised by dragging. Every viewer surveyed below has groups. This design adds them as one more row kind in volna-core. The row operations that exist today (selection, drag, clipboard, heights and workspace entries) extend to them, and one new painter draws a folded group. Groups are presentation, so they are stored in the workspace and never in VTR. Group templates from VDB profiles are postponed. Related documents: workspaces.html (the workspace file), c910-perf-counters.html (row styles that need group rows), analog-waves.html and transaction-waveforms.html (other row kinds on the same canvas).

How it behaves

  
    
    
  1. A group is a row.It has a name, a folded flag and a height, and it sits in the same list as signals, lanes and clocks. Selection, dragging, cut, copy, paste, removal, heights and the workspace entry treat it like any other row. A group may be empty. Group names do not need to be unique, because a group is not an address: it is found by its place in the list.
    2. 
    
  2. Groups nest up to eight levels.Each level indents the name by 14 px, and a thin guide runs down the left of an open group. Surfer, Questa, SimVision and Vivado all nest. Eight levels hold a hierarchy that deep, and the fixed limit keeps the name column readable. Deeper structure is refused when it is created and when a workspace is loaded.
    3. 
    
  3. Two explicit ways to make one.G (or Group selection in the row menu) puts the selected rows, in their order, under a new group at the place of the first one, and starts renaming it. + group on a sidebar scope adds that scope's variables under a group named after the scope. Its child scopes become folded subgroups. Volna never groups rows on its own. The same trace always opens with the same rows, and grouping happens only when you ask for it.
    4. 
    
  4. Fold and unfold.Click the chevron, or use ← and → as in the scope tree. In Volna these keys pan, so they fold and unfold only while the selected row is a group; the mock also uses them to walk the tree. Alt applies to every group inside as well, and Fold all and Unfold all apply to the whole panel. When a folded group hides selected rows, the group itself becomes the selection, so no command acts on rows you cannot see. Folding changes what is visible, never what is loaded: the rows keep their histories and their formats.
    5. 
    
  5. A folded group still shows its signals' activity.Its row is drawn in the shape of a bus. A box boundary marks every time any signal in the group changes. A column with several changes becomes a solid band. Where any signal is undefined, the row is drawn in the X colour. The value column reads “9 signals” or, when the cursor sits on a change, “2 of 9 changed”, followed by the number of undefined signals. Hovering the row lists the signals' values under the pointer. The next section explains why this summary and not another.
    6. 
    
  6. Row commands take the whole group.Moving, copying, cutting, removing and Open in table act on the group and everything in it. Commands about values, such as the format menu, T and A for analog, apply to the signals in the group that accept them. Commands about the row itself, such as height, apply only to the group's own row. Selecting a group selects only its row, and the commands then expand it to its subtree. A row inside the group can still be selected on its own.
    7. 
    
  7. Drag with a level.Dragging names shows the insertion line indented to the level at which the rows will land. Between two rows there is usually more than one valid level. For example, below the last row of a group the rows can go into that group or after it. The pointer's horizontal position chooses among the valid levels, as in Surfer. Dropping on the middle half of a folded group's row appends the rows to that group and leaves it folded. A group can never be dropped inside itself.
    8. 
    
  8. Ungroup keeps the rows, Remove takes them.Shift+G removes the group row and moves its children up one level, in the same place and order. Delete removes the group with everything in it, and the row menu says so (Remove with contents). Surfer, Questa and Vivado draw the same line between the two. Cut followed by paste is the way to put a removed group back until Volna has undo.
    9. 
    
  9. Navigation works on folded groups.Shift+→ on a group steps the cursor to the next change of any signal in it. A click on its waves snaps to those changes. Open in table opens the group's signals as a multi-signal table. So a folded group is a working view, not just a place to hide rows.
    10. 
    
  10. Saved with the panel.A workspace saves each panel's rows as a tree, including names and fold state. Splitting a panel copies its groups. The clipboard keeps the structure, so a pasted group arrives whole, under the last selected row, at that row's level. Rows whose signals the trace no longer contains stay in their group as unresolved rows, as they do today at the top level.
    11. 
  






  
What a folded group shows

  
This is where the surveyed viewers differ most. Surfer, Vivado and GTKWave's plain groups draw nothing: the row is a heading. SimVision's manual says a collapsed group shows “the combined waveforms” next to its name. Perfetto draws a summary track that the group owns, and in its slice mode that summary has up to eight mini-lanes. GTKWave's composite groups show the value of the vector the member bits form. The three figures draw the same folded Write group (seven signals) over the whole trace.

  

    
    
    
  

  

  
Why the bus shape. A folded group answers two questions: did anything in here happen, and is anything in here broken? The union of the members' change times answers the first, and the union of their undefined stretches answers the second. Both come from the histories the rows already hold. Drawn as a bus row with no labels, the summary reuses the existing bus painter, its dense-column bands and the X colours. It adds no new visual language, and it works for groups that mix bits, buses, enums and lanes. The value cell and the hover list give the detail, without unfolding the group.

  
Cost. The proposal had the painter ask each member's history for its first change per pixel column with the same galloping search the bus painter uses: O(W·k·log) per frame for k members, with a merged summary on the load worker if a 1,000-member group took more than 4 ms. It took 261 ms zoomed out over 107 changes, so the summary was built. The painter now walks the members' visible changes when there are at most 16,384, and otherwise reads a summary of change counts and undefined bits per block of time, like the analog block summary in analog-waves.html; the same frame costs 0.1 ms. The members of a folded group load like any other rows. The memory ledger already refuses loads that exceed the budget, and the summary draws unloaded members as missing, not as quiet.

  
Richer summaries later. c910-perf-counters.html proposes row styles that draw a group of signals on one scale: stacked area, heat lanes, horizon group and overlay. They are group rows with a style, and this design gives them their data model. A group gains a draw setting next to its fold state, and the activity summary is its default. These styles are drawn only while the group is folded or when the row is taller than 1×. They are deferred until the plain groups exist.

    •

Rejected
    
      
  • A blank folded row (Surfer, Vivado, GTKWave). It hides activity.
    • 
      
  • Begin and end marker rows with repair passes (GTKWave). The tree cannot be malformed by construction.
    • 
      
  • Hidden selection of children (GTKWave). Commands name what they do.
    • 
      
  • Grouping by region implicitly and reusing that group on the next Ctrl+G (Questa). The result depends on what you did before.
    • 
      
  • Repeating the group on every saved line (Questa wave.do), and order-dependent scripts (.svcf, signal.rc).
    • 
      
  • Two copies of state, such as Perfetto's pinned clones beside the tree, or Surfer's dead content and is_open fields.
    • 
      
  • Meaningless default names that stick. G starts a rename right away.
    • 
      
  • Bundling bits into a vector as a kind of group (GTKWave composites). A derived signal is a translator or VDB feature, not a row container.
    • 
      
  • Absolute database-qualified member names (SimVision's simulator::, Vivado's db_ref_id).

The data model in volna-core

  

  
The rows of a WaveModel stay a vector. Each entry gains a depth, and a fourth row kind is added. The vector is the tree in pre-order: an entry's subtree is the entry plus every following entry that is deeper than it. This is Surfer's representation, and it fits Volna's existing code. Row heights are prefix sums over a vector. Selection is a set of indices. Clipboard ranges and moves are splices. None of them needs pointers or ids.

  


// wave/model.rs
pub enum WaveRow { Signal(DisplayedSignal), Lane(TxLane), Clock(ClockRow), Group(GroupRow) }

pub struct GroupRow {
    pub name: String,
    pub collapsed: bool,
    pub height: RowHeight,              // the group's own row; 1× by default
}

pub struct Entry { pub depth: u8, pub row: WaveRow }  // replaces bare WaveRow in the list

pub struct WaveModel {
    pub items: Vec<Entry>,              // pre-order tree
    pub selected: BTreeSet<usize>,      // entry indices, always visible ones
    visible: Arc<[u32]>,                // derived: entry index of each visible row
    …
}

// wave/tree.rs — pure functions over &[Entry], unit-tested headlessly
pub const MAX_DEPTH: u8 = 8;
pub fn validate(items: &[Entry]) -> Result<()>;               // depth(i+1) ≤ depth(i) + [i is a group]; first is 0
pub fn subtree(items: &[Entry], i: usize) -> Range<usize>;
pub fn parent(items: &[Entry], i: usize) -> Option<usize>;
pub fn visible(items: &[Entry]) -> Arc<[u32]>;                // skips subtrees of folded groups
pub fn roots(items: &[Entry], sel: &BTreeSet<usize>) -> Vec<usize>;  // selected, no selected ancestor
pub fn gap_depths(items: &[Entry], visible: &[u32], gap: usize) -> RangeInclusive<u8>;
pub fn plan_move(items: &[Entry], sel: &BTreeSet<usize>, to: Drop) -> Option<Moved>;
pub fn group(items: &mut Vec<Entry>, sel: &BTreeSet<usize>, name: String) -> Option<usize>;
pub fn ungroup(items: &mut Vec<Entry>, groups: &BTreeSet<usize>) -> BTreeSet<usize>;
pub fn leaves(items: &[Entry], i: usize) -> impl Iterator<Item = usize>;

  
  

  
Two index spaces. Stored indices address entries, and visible positions address painted rows. WaveLayout computes row tops over the visible rows only. Its rows range, the hit tests and the scrollbar therefore work unchanged on visible positions, and one lookup in visible turns a hit into an entry. visible is derived. It is rebuilt with the row tops whenever the list or a fold flag changes. It is never saved and never edited by hand, so it has no consistency rule of its own.

  
What changes in existing code.

  
    
    
  • add_vars, add_lanes and add_clocks append entries at depth 0.
    • 
    
  • remove_selected, copy_selected and move_selected_to operate on the subtrees of roots(selected).
    • 
    
  • Drag::Rows gains a depth and an into for the drop target (the target type is tree::Place in the code, since Drop is a Rust trait). move_target becomes plan_move, which returns None for a move into itself, a move that is too deep or a move that changes nothing. As today, None hides the drop line.
    • 
    
  • Document::copied_rows becomes Vec<Entry>, normalised to depth 0.
    • 
    
  • EdgeSource gains Group, a merge over the leaves' sources. It is used by edge stepping and click snapping.
    • 
    
  • The menus gain MenuAction::{Group, Ungroup, Rename, Fold, FoldAll, UnfoldAll}. Action gains GroupSelection, Ungroup and RenameGroup. The ←/→ fold keys go through the pan actions, which fold only a selected group.
    • 
    
  • Commands that set a format or analog drawing on a selected group map it to leaves().
    • 
  

  
Renaming is core state: WaveModel::rename: Option<usize> and a rename_rect() in the layout. The GPUI frontend hosts a text input over that rectangle, as it hosts popup menus today, and commits the text with Command::RenameGroup. egui gets the Scene for free and can leave renaming unimplemented: it must still build, and parity is not required.

  
Accessibility. The GPUI canvas exposes table rows as a list box today. Wave rows become an AccessKit Tree of TreeItems, each with its level, its expanded state (groups only) and its selection, built from visible. The planned browser assertion of this tree is not possible yet: gpui's web platform has no AccessKit adapter, so the tree is tested through WaveModel::accessible_rows and the canvas's node mapping.

  
Nothing in the session layer changes. The session serves histories by signal, and a group is only an arrangement of rows. Local and remote sessions are unaffected, and so is the query protocol.

  






  
Where groups are stored

  

  
The workspace is the source of truth for what a panel shows. The waves panel entry moves to version 4. Its rows become a tree, and a group entry holds its children. On load, the tree is flattened into entries, and the depth limit and the row limit (MAX_ROWS, which counts groups too) are checked in the same pass. Structure therefore cannot be malformed on disk, unlike GTKWave's marker rows, and cannot be merely flagged as invalid, as Surfer's flat list with levels can. selected keeps storing stored indices. A version 3 panel is not migrated: like any panel version the core does not know, it is reported as unsupported and kept in the file unchanged (PanelKind::Unsupported).

  


{ "kind": "waves", "version": 4, …,
  "rows": [
    {"type": "signal", "signal": ["soc", "clk"], "format": "bin"},
    {"type": "group", "name": "AXI master", "rows": [
      {"type": "group", "name": "Write", "rows": [ … ]},
      {"type": "group", "name": "Read", "collapsed": true, "height": 2, "rows": [ … ]}
    ]}
  ] }

  

  
Never in VTR, never in the protocol, not in settings. VTR already carries the design hierarchy, which Add scope as group uses. A group is an arrangement made by a person, and the README's VTR/VDB boundary keeps presentation out of the trace. Remote sessions keep carrying raw trace data only. settings.json holds preferences, not per-design content. Reusing a group across traces or designs is left to VDB profile templates.

  






  
Implementation plan

  
Steps 1–8 are implemented with headless tests in volna-core (src/wave/{tree,group}.rs, tests/groups.rs) and GPUI tests in volna/src/app_tests.rs; step 9 is deferred. None of it touches VTR, the C API or the benchmarks. Where the work differs from the plan, the row says so.

Open questions

  
    
    
  • Should Delete on a large group (say more than 100 rows) ask first while Volna has no undo? The proposal says no: the menu names the action, and cut and paste can recover it. Undo is the better fix for every row command.
    • 
    
  • Should a folded group defer loading its members until it is unfolded or its summary is on screen? That would help scope groups with thousands of signals. Lazy loading of off-screen rows applies to flat lists too, so it should be designed once, for both.
    • 
    
  • Should Add scope as group include child scopes by default, or only when asked, as Surfer's two menu entries do? The implementation offers both, with child scopes (folded) as the first entry.
    • 
    
  • Group colours and dividers are left out. An empty folded group already works as a labelled divider. Colour is a per-row feature that Volna does not have yet for any row kind.
    • 
  

  






  
Postponed: group templates from VDB profiles

  
This part waits until Volna can attach a VDB profile, which it cannot do yet. Nothing above depends on it: the groups, their workspace entries and Add scope as group work without a profile. It is recorded here so that the workspace format and the unresolved-row behaviour leave room for it.

  

  
Templates, not live groups. The VDB application note already lists “signal groups” under the waveform view of an RTL debugger VDB. A profile names group templates by relative names and a scope selector. Examples are the channels of an AXI port, a CSR bank, or a pipeline stage's valid/ready pairs.

  


// soc.vdb.json — a profile document, next to the trace or in the VDB repository
{ "groups": [
  { "id": "axi_write", "name": "AXI write", "applies_to": "**.u_axi*",
    "rows": ["awvalid", "awready", "awaddr", "awprot", "wvalid", "wready", "wdata", "wstrb", "bvalid"] }
] }

  

  
A template appears in the sidebar's scope menu when its selector matches the scope. Insert resolves the names against the scope and copies the resulting group into the panel, where it becomes ordinary workspace rows. Names the trace lacks become unresolved rows, so the group keeps its intended shape and the gap shows. The copy is not linked back to the template. Editing a live-linked group would raise the question whose change wins, and Perfetto's workspaces make the same choice: they clone. Relative names and a selector make a template reusable across instances and designs, which is what Verdi's $$top is for.

  

  
When it is picked up, the work is: template selectors in the profile format (docs/VDB_APPNOTE.md), an Insert entry in the scope tree's menu for matching templates, and tests that missing names become unresolved rows inside the inserted group.

Transactions as rows in the waveform panel

Add a transaction generator to the Waves panel the way you add a signal. It becomes one more row: each transaction is a bar over its lifetime, lined up with the handshakes that produced it.

Design proposal, not implemented. Transactions are visible today only in the Pipeline, Table and Transaction panels. Next to the signals, you can answer questions such as which read owns this rvalid plateau? without comparing two panels. The proposal builds on the Transaction panel, per-row heights and the row clipboard that Volna already has. Related documents: pipeline-view.html, tx-detail.html, hierarchy.html.

Try it

This is a mock of the Waves panel with one AXI read generator, axi_rd, added as a lane below its channel signals. The trace is generated for this page. It opens with four hand-written reads that overlap. One read waits on the address channel, and one meets backpressure and ends in SLVERR. After them come about a hundred generated reads over 6 µs. How it works

  1. A lane is a row.A lane sits in the panel's row list next to the signals. It supports the same row operations: select, drag to reorder, cut, copy and paste, the Height menu, and Remove. Workspaces save it like any other row. To add one, choose Add to Waves on a generator in the hierarchy browser. Double-clicking a generator still opens the Pipeline panel.
  2. Each bar is a transaction's lifetime.A bar runs from begin to end. The address and data stages are solid and the wait between them is faded, so a stall shows at a glance. Errors are red. When a bar is wide enough, it shows the transaction id and address. Overlapping transactions stack into sub-rows. The stacking is computed once when the generator loads, so bars never move while you pan.
  3. Lanes use ordinary row heights.A new lane gets the smallest height preset that fits its deepest overlap, up to 4×. If you choose a smaller height, the deeper sub-rows fold into the bottom one. Hatching marks where folded bars overlap, and the name cell shows how many sub-rows are folded.
  4. Zoomed out, the lane becomes a density strip.When a typical transaction would be narrower than about 4 px, bars are no longer readable. The lane then draws one column per pixel. Column height shows how many transactions are open at that time, and a red cap means at least one of them failed. Zoom in and the bars return.
  5. Selection is shared with the other panels.Clicking a lane works like clicking a signal: the cursor moves to the pointer and snaps to the nearest transaction boundary. If the click lands on a bar, that transaction becomes the document selection, the same selection the Pipeline and Table panels set. The Transaction panel then shows it, and every other panel highlights it. Clicking empty lane space clears the selection. Enter or a double-click opens the Transaction panel.
  6. Everything else stays the same.Signal rows, zoom, pan, markers and Ctrl+drag range zoom work as they do today. The value column lists the transactions open at the cursor. On a signal, Shift+←/→ jumps to the previous or next value change. On a lane, the same keys jump between transaction begins and ends.

What it builds on

Most of the machinery already exists, so the lane needs one new row kind and one painter. Loading, window queries, heights, selection and the detail view are shared with panels that ship today.

Window queriesexistsLoadedGenerator::visit_window, the max-end interval tree. Painting, hit testing and density bins share one visit per frame, so the work is bounded by the visible bars rather than by the size of the generator.

Edge navigationchangednext_edge and prev_edge ask the selected row for its next boundary. A lane answers with transaction begins and ends.
PaintingnewA lane painter that emits Scene quads and text through the existing overlay helpers. It is covered by headless scene tests.
Adding a lanenewAn Add to Waves item for generators in the hierarchy browser. activate_members keeps opening the Pipeline panel.

Nothing is added to VTR or to the client–server protocol. Stacking, folding, density and colours are viewer decisions, computed in volna-core over the immutable transaction records. A remote lane loads through the same LoadRequest::Track under the client memory budget. volna-egui only has to build against the new row enum.

Why a row

Today you can dock a Pipeline panel under the waves and link the two viewports. That is the right tool for stage analysis and long instruction streams. It is the wrong tool for correlating transactions with signals, because the transactions sit in another panel, at another row pitch, one row per transaction. Your eye has to carry every time position across a panel border.

A lane puts the lifetime directly beside the signals and inherits the panel's viewport, cursor, markers and gestures, with nothing new to keep in sync. The Pipeline panel stays the tool for stage-level questions.

Two other shapes were prototyped and rejected:

  • A lane attached under a signal group. This needs row groups with a durable anchor identity. Its best idea, highlighting the related signals of the selected transaction, can be added later as a selection overlay on the plain row.
  • A transaction shelf at the bottom of the waves panel. This is a second panel inside the first, with its own sash, scrolling and persistence. Docking a Pipeline panel below the waves already produces that layout.

Plan

  1. Validate first. Compare the lane with linked Waves and Pipeline panels on two tasks over a real AXI trace: find the stalled read's address, and find the read that failed. If the linked panels do as well, do not build the lane.
  2. Row model. Add WaveRow, the lane state, move_rows, a clipboard of WaveRows and workspace entries. Unresolved lanes restore as empty rows, like unresolved signals.
  3. Painting. Draw bars, stage shading, folding and the density strip, with headless scene tests.
  4. Interaction. Add the hit test, click snapping, TxSelection, edge navigation, the row menu and Add to Waves.
  5. Measure. Time sub-row stacking and paint per frame on the largest generators available: pipeline_showcase.vtr and a Verilator AXI trace with 10⁴–10⁶ transactions. Compare against a build without lanes and record the results in docs/RATIONALE.md.

Open questions

  • Is per-frame density binning fast enough at 10⁶ transactions, or does the lane need a persistent per-generator summary?
  • Is “narrower than 4 px” the right point to switch to the density strip, or should it be a per-lane choice?
  • Should dragging a generator from the hierarchy onto the waves also add a lane?

Later

Possible follow-ups: several generators in one lane, relation arrows between bars, bar labels and colours from VDB profiles, and highlighting the related signal rows of the selected transaction.

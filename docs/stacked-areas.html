Stacked areas for counters that add up

Draw a group of counter signals as one absolute stacked area. Each part gets its own colour, the top edge is the total, and zero stays at the bottom. The row shows how much work there was and what it was made of. Groups whose parts do not add up are refused.

Proposal, 28 September 2026. This is the first presentation feature of c910-perf-counters.html, whose plan lists stacked areas first among the new row styles, for the top-down slots and the eight issue pipes. It builds on wave_groups.html (a group is a row), analog-waves.html (numbers from translators, per-column drawing, the analog summary) and undo-redo.html (cockpit edits). The 100% stack, heat lanes and horizons stay in the parent proposal's later steps. The demo does not use mock data. It shows the per-pipe issue counts and top-down slots that bench/workloads/c910/perf_metrics.py computed from the full-design openC910 CoreMark recording, copied from the parent page's embedded data. Nothing described here is implemented in Volna yet.

When a stack is the right chart

An absolute stack answers two questions at once: how large the total is, and which parts make it up. It is honest only when the top edge is a quantity someone would ask about, so the parts must add up. Volna draws a group as a stack only when its members meet these four conditions.

  1. One unit and one denominator. Instructions per cycle add to instructions per cycle, and entries add to entries. Entries do not add to bytes per cycle. Volna checks this wherever units are declared.
  2. Disjoint parts. No event and no entry may be counted in two parts. Every in-flight load holds both a ROB entry and a load queue entry, so rob.entries plus lsu.lq counts each load twice. No viewer can see this from the values, so the VDB profile vouches for the groups it declares.
  3. No negative parts. A negative part pulls every layer above it down, and the total becomes a difference. Volna checks this from each member's minimum over the trace. Diverging stacks for signed parts are a later step.
  4. Still additive after averaging. When zoomed out, a pixel column shows the mean over its time span, and means over the same span add up. This holds for per-cycle counts, for levels, and for rates per cycle. It fails for ratios with another denominator. The cycle-weighted mean of windowed misses per kilo-instruction is not the MPKI of the span, and a sum of such means is not either. Stacks of such ratios wait for derived signals, which store the numerator and the denominator and divide in the viewer.

The counter catalogue has several good fits: issue.pipe0…7 (instructions per cycle by pipe), the top-down slots (a partition of four slots per cycle), bus.ar.{dfill, ifill, iprefetch, dprefetch, uncached} (bus reads by requester) and the line-fill buffer split by source. The issue queues are a poor fit even though they pass every check. Their parts are disjoint and share a unit, so the total is correct, but the question people ask about queues is which one is full, and a stack hides each queue's capacity. Heat lanes or separate rows answer that question better. When the parts matter more than the total, a stack is the wrong chart. Only the bottom layer and the total are read against the axis. Every other layer is a length between two curves (see research).

How it works

  1. A stack is a way to draw a group.Choose Stacked area in the Draw section of the group's badge menu, or press Shift+A with the group selected. The group's own row becomes the plot and grows to 4×. Its member rows stay below it and act as the legend, with a colour swatch, the name and the value at the cursor. Folding the group hides the legend and keeps the plot. Everything else about groups works as before: selecting, moving, cutting, pasting, renaming and ungrouping. A still toggles the members' own analog plots, as it does for any group today.
  2. It checks the group before drawing.Stacked area is available only when every member is numeric in its format and never negative over the trace, and, where units are declared, when all members share one unit. Otherwise the menu entry is disabled and gives the reason, and Shift+A puts the reason in the status line. When the VDB profile declares a partition, meaning parts that always sum to a fixed capacity such as the four rename slots, Volna verifies it. The header then reads “sums to 4 ✓”, or it names the first time where the sum differs. Double counting cannot be detected. A stack built from signals whose units are not declared says “units not declared” in its readout.
  3. The order is the list order.The legend lists the layers top to bottom in the same order as the plot. The last member sits on the baseline. Move a member with the usual row drag, or with Move to baseline or Move to top in its menu. Only the baseline layer and the total are read against the axis, so the baseline should hold the part that matters most. Volna never reorders layers by value, because a layer that changes position as its size changes cannot be followed.
  4. Colours follow the signal.A member keeps its colour slot when it is reordered or hidden. Slots come from the VDB profile or, for a new stack, from list order. There are eight slots from a categorical palette, and every adjacent pair has been checked for separation under colour-vision deficiency. A ninth member does not get a generated hue. Members beyond eight are merged into one neutral “other” layer on top, and the readout lists them. A one-pixel gap in the plot's surface colour separates the layers. They have no borders.
  5. Zero at the bottom, one scale.The baseline is always zero, because a stack with a cut baseline misstates every part. The top of the scale works like an analog range. The default is Whole trace, the maximum of the total over the recording rounded up to a clean number, so the plot does not move while you pan. Capacity is offered when the profile declares one (8 issue pipes, 4 slots) and is the default for a declared partition, whose total always equals it. Visible window eases to the visible maximum as you pan. A declared capacity is drawn as a dashed line whenever it is in range. From 2× up, the row labels its top value, and the header names the unit and the number of parts.
  6. Reading values.The cursor puts a dot on the total. The group's value cell shows the sum Σ, and each legend row shows its member's value. Hovering the plot lists every part from top to bottom with its value and share, followed by the total. The layer under the pointer is highlighted and the others are dimmed. In a column that covers many cycles, the readout says that its values are means over the column's cycles and adds the column's peak total. Shift+←/→ steps through the changes of the total.
  7. Hiding a layer.Click a swatch or choose Hide from stack. The layer leaves the plot and the total but keeps its colour and its legend row, which shows a hollow swatch. The header then says “Σ of 6 of 8”, so a partial sum is not mistaken for the whole. The partition check is suspended while a part is hidden.
  8. Edits are undoable; looking is not.The draw style, the range, the peak band, the layer order and hidden layers are stored in the workspace, and each change is a labelled step in the journal. The demo's status line names the step, and Ctrl+Z reverts it. Hover, cursor, zoom and folding are navigation and never make a step.
  9. Undefined and missing values.Members change at different times. The stack is built on the union of their change times, never by sample index. Where a member is X or Z, or has no value yet, the layers below it are drawn and the rest of the column is painted in the undefined colour. Its readout shows X, and so does the total. A missing value is never read as zero.

Zoomed out: means add up, extremes do not

Both figures stack the eight issue pipes of the list stretch, 1,024 cycles in 64 columns of 16 cycles each. The white line in both is the true peak of the total in each column, the largest number of instructions issued in any one of its cycles.

The analog painter keeps a line accurate at any zoom by drawing the first, last, minimum and maximum value of each pixel column (M4). That method does not carry over to stacks, because the maximum of a sum is not the sum of the maxima. Jugel et al. leave area charts to future work, and no choice of per-layer extremes gives a correct stack.

Means do add up. Over one column's time span, the time-weighted mean of the total equals the sum of the members' means, so a stack of means is exact. Its area is the number of events, its top is the column's average total, and each layer is its member's average. For a per-cycle count, that mean is the event rate in events per cycle. Means hide bursts, so a faint band above the stack reaches the column's peak total. The option is Peak of total, and it is on by default. The band's top is the total's own maximum, not a sum of per-layer maxima. When a column holds one step or less, the peak equals the top of the stack and the band vanishes.

The painter chooses between two paths each frame, as the analog painter does. If the total has fewer visible steps than a quarter of the plot width, every step is drawn exactly at its time, from a merge of the members' visible changes. Otherwise the painter draws one column per pixel. A member's mean over a column is its integral at the column's right edge minus the integral at its left edge, divided by the column's duration. Integrals come from prefix sums, so a frame costs about members × width × log(changes), however many changes are on screen. The peak band needs the total's maximum per column, which no member summary can provide. For long histories, a total summary stores the total's minimum and maximum per block of time, like GroupSummary, built in one merged pass on the load worker. Its minimum also serves the partition check.

What research and other tools say

Perception. Cleveland and McGill (1984) discuss this chart directly, as the aggregate line chart with curves A, A+B, A+B+C and the total. They note that only A and the total can be judged by position along a common scale, while the other parts must be judged as vertical lengths between two curves, which they call inaccurate and harder than on a divided bar chart. Their advice is to plot the parts directly. Byron and Wattenberg (2008) write that a zero baseline makes the sum easy to read, possibly at the expense of the individual layers. They rule out gaps that would distort the sum, so a change in a lower layer makes every layer above it wiggle. Their ordering advice, to place stable series where their wiggle disturbs the fewest others, means the bottom when the baseline is zero. They also ask for enough local contrast to tell layers apart without heavy borders. Thudt et al. (GI 2016) found no performance advantage for the zero-baseline stack in any condition. Readers nevertheless rated it the most readable and the most pleasing. Wilke's Fundamentals of Data Visualization makes the same point about stacked densities: the middle and top parts are hard to compare because they are shifted against each other, and a separate plot per part helps. Javed et al. (2010) is often cited in this context, but it tested line, braided, small-multiple and horizon charts, not stacks.

These findings shape rules 3, 4 and 6. The baseline layer is chosen deliberately, the gaps are one pixel of surface colour rather than borders, and every part has an exact value in its legend row. The members also remain ordinary rows, so a part is one action away from its own plot.

Rejected: stacking by sample index (ECharts), because members of a VTR group change at different times. Interpolation and inferred zeros (Plotly), because both invent values the trace does not hold. Deferred: diverging stacks for signed parts, and the normalized (100%) stack, which is the parent proposal's next row style.

Where it lives

Almost all of the work belongs in volna-core: one group style, one module of numbers, one painter, two summary extensions and the commands. volna binds the key and shows the menu sections. volna-egui only has to build, and it draws a stacked group as it draws any group today. VTR and the client–server protocol do not change, because stacks are presentation over immutable histories that local and remote sessions already deliver.

Headless verification

Every check is automated and runs in CI without a display.

  1. Core property test, volna-core/tests/stack.rs. It generates random member histories with change times that do not line up, X stretches and late starts, then picks random viewports and widths. The column means must sum to the total's mean within 10⁻⁹ and never exceed the column peak. The exact and column paths must agree when each column holds at most one change. Summary integrals must equal a direct walk. Refusals must be correct for mixed units, negative parts and non-numeric formats. A declared partition must be verified, hidden members must be left out of the total, and the readout must list the members in list order.
  2. Scene tests. A three-member history renders its layers in order with gaps, an undefined member paints the undefined colour above its layer, and the peak band reaches the true maximum.
  3. History and workspace. The model-based test in tests/history.rs gains style, range, peak, hide and move-layer operations. A workspace round trip at version 5 must restore every stack setting, and a version 4 file must fail with a clear error.
  4. GPUI. app_tests.rs checks that shift-a toggles a stack, that the menu shows the reason for a refusal, and that a swatch click hides a layer.
  5. Cost. examples/stack_cost.rs times a zoomed-out frame of eight members at 10⁶ and 10⁷ changes, with the same 4 ms budget the analog painter was held to. The results and the summary block size go into docs/RATIONALE.md.
  6. This page. node --test docs/tests/stacked-areas.test.mjs drives the demo in headless Chrome at desktop and phone widths through guides, pointer and keys. It asserts states, readouts and rendered pixel colours, checks that the embedded data matches the parent page's data, and runs the page's self-test. .github/workflows/stacked-areas.yml runs it on every change.

Risks

  • Stacks of things that do not add. The checks catch mixed units, negative parts and broken partitions. They cannot catch double counting. Declared VDB templates and the “units not declared” readout reduce this risk, but they do not remove it.
  • Upper layers are hard to read. Their values are lengths, not positions. The legend values, the hover readout and a deliberate baseline choice mitigate this, and a part can always be plotted as its own row.
  • Means hide bursts. The peak band, the exact path when zoomed in, and a readout that says “mean” keep a zoomed-out stack from looking calmer than the machine was.
  • Thin layers vanish. A layer below a pixel is invisible, as the FPU pipes are here. The readout still lists it, and hiding idle layers clears the legend.
  • Colour limits. The green slot has a contrast of 2.8:1 against the dark plot surface, below WCAG's 3:1 for graphics. The gaps, the legend text and the readout carry the same information, which WCAG 1.4.11 accepts, and colour is never the only cue. The light theme uses the palette's light steps, where magenta, yellow and aqua need the same relief.
  • Cost with many members. Without summaries, a folded 1,000-signal group cost 261 ms per frame. Stacks are capped at eight coloured layers, but the members' integrals still scale with their histories. The cost example sets the gate before the feature ships.

Plan

  1. Numbers and checks. Write wave/stack.rs with the steps, columns, readout and check, together with the property test.
  2. Row style and commands. Add the group style, the member layer, workspace version 5, the commands and journal steps, the painter with its scene tests, and the GPUI key and menu sections.
  3. Summaries and measurement. Add block integrals, the total summary and stack_cost, and record the numbers in docs/RATIONALE.md.
  4. C910 templates. Once VDB profiles attach to Volna, declare the issue and topdown stacks in c910.perf.vdb.json for the parent proposal's starter set.

Open questions

  • Should Shift+A stack a group, or should stacking be available only from the menu?
  • Is 4× the right height for a new stack? Analog rows open at 3×.
  • Should a stack whose units are not declared need a confirmation, or is the readout note enough?

Later

Follow-ups, roughly in the parent proposal's order: the 100% stack for shares, diverging stacks for signed parts, a hover gesture that temporarily moves a layer to the baseline (Thudt et al. found that straightening a layer helped), derived signals for stacks of ratios, and heat lanes for groups whose parts should not be summed.
